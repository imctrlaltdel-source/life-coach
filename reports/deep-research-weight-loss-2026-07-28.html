The scale is misleading. Of the 1.4 kg "lost," most is muscle mass; net fat mass is essentially unchanged (~19 kg → ~20 kg). This is not weight loss failure — it is structural muscle catabolism disguised as weight loss.

The three drivers:

  1. Protein ceiling: Kidney limits intake to ~90 g/day (≈1.1 g/kg). Muscle preservation in deficit requires 1.6–2.4 g/kg (Morton 2018 meta-analysis). PJ is structurally below the minimum effective dose. Muscle loss is thermodynamically expected.
  2. Metformin + low protein: Metformin inhibits mTORC1; MASTERS trial (Walton 2019) showed ~50% blunting of resistance-training hypertrophy. Compounds the protein deficit.
  3. Defended visceral fat: Chronic stress → 11β-HSD1 upregulation in omental fat → visceral depot resists deficit. Needs 8–12 weeks of clean deficit without weekend spikes (Sci Reports 2024). PJ has not had this window — cannabis nights and midnight eating events reset the clock.

The BMR drop (−21 kcal in 83 days) is the metabolic tell: the body has responded to sustained deficit by reducing energy expenditure — classic adaptive thermogenesis, worsened by muscle loss (each kg lost muscle ≈ 13 kcal/day BMR).

3. Recommendations by Vector

Vector A — Medication (most impactful — do this first)

A1. Identify antihypertensive class TODAY

If PJ is on a beta-blocker (metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol, propranolol, carvedilol): this alone could explain 30–40% of the fat retention. β-blockers reduce lipolysis via β-adrenergic blockade, cut exercise fat oxidation ~13%, and cause 1.2–3.4 kg weight gain over 12 months.

IJO 2010, meta-analysis: β-blockers & weight gain / exercise capacity.

Fix: For T2D + hypertension + nephropathy, guideline standard is an ARB (Telmisartan / Losartan) — renoprotective (RENAAL, IDNT trials) and metabolically neutral or favourable. Ask cardiologist/nephrologist to switch if currently on β-blocker.

A2. Add SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin or dapagliflozin)

Triple benefit for PJ's exact profile:

  • Renal protection (EMPA-REG OUTCOME; DAPA-CKD) — directly addresses proteinuria 2+.
  • Fat loss: −2.1 kg fat / +0.8 kg muscle at 52 weeks in T2D patients.
  • HbA1c reduction 0.5–0.8% without hypoglycaemia.

PMC11597755, 52-week prospective study; EMPA-REG OUTCOME (NEJM 2015).

Caveats to discuss with doctor: euglycaemic DKA risk (rare); genitourinary infections; hold on sick days.

A3. Semaglutide / GLP-1 agonist — AVOID

Contraindicated given active NPDR. SUSTAIN-6 showed semaglutide associated with ~3× risk of diabetic retinopathy progression (HR 1.76, 95% CI 1.11–2.78) vs placebo. Rapid glucose lowering in NPDR eyes = risk of PDR progression = vision loss. The weight-loss benefit does not outweigh the retinal risk.

SUSTAIN-6 (NEJM 2016); AAO position statement on GLP-1 & retinopathy.

A4. Metformin — continue, but raise protein to full ceiling every day

Metformin's mTORC1 inhibition is amplified when protein intake is below 1.2 g/kg. PJ is at 0.60 g/kg on rest days. The interaction is the problem, not the drug.

Walton 2019, MASTERS trial, Aging Cell.

A5. Uric acid — recheck; consider allopurinol

UA 8.1 mg/dL is above symptomatic gout threshold AND inhibits AMPK → suppresses fat oxidation. Retest; if still ≥7, discuss allopurinol 100–300 mg with nephrologist (also renoprotective in CKD per Bardin 2017).

Current: 47.8 g rest / 61.6 g gym  →  Target: 80–90 g EVERY day

PJ cannot reach the muscle-preservation optimum (1.6 g/kg = 126 g) because of kidney disease. He will continue losing muscle in deficit. The only lever is to minimise the loss by hitting the kidney ceiling every single day, not just gym days.

  • 5 servings/day = BF + Lunch + 4pm snack + Dinner + pre-bed.

Vector C — Circadian & Cannabis

C1. Cannabis — 30 day full pause

Even episodic use (1–3×/week) is doing more damage than PJ realises:

  • THC → acute ghrelin surge → +400–800 cal same night (typically the midnight-eating events).
  • REM + slow-wave sleep suppression on use nights → next-day GLP-1/PYY drop → +300 cal next day.
  • 2 events/week × 600 cal + 2 next-days × 300 cal = ~1,800 cal/wk = 7,200 cal/month. That is the entire monthly deficit target, erased.

Transl Psychiatry (THC → ghrelin RCT); Cannabis & Sleep meta-analysis 2025, ScienceDirect.

C2. Kitchen closes at 9PM — hard rule

Post-10PM calories shift skeletal muscle gene expression toward fat storage independent of caloric total (PNAS 2018). This is not "calories in vs out" — it is a direct molecular fat-storage signal.

  • If genuine hunger after 10PM → 300 ml salted buttermilk only.
  • All party/dinner-out food must finish by 9:30PM.

PNAS 2018 (Wefers et al., circadian misalignment → muscle fatty acid genes); PubMed 2019 late-night adipogenesis.

C3. Weekend sleep protection

One night of 4–5h sleep = +300–500 cal Monday intake, driven by GLP-1/PYY drop and elevated ghrelin. PJ's weekend social events + late nights predict Monday hyperphagia — which the logs confirm.

  • Saturday: lights out by 11:30 PM regardless of social plans.
  • Sunday: 7+ hrs sleep, no exceptions.

PMC7071396 sleep restriction & satiety hormones RCT.

Vector D — Exercise Modification (counter-intuitive)

D1. Drop Max HR to ≤138 bpm — MAF protocol

Current: 64% of gym sessions Max HR >150. Range 150–167. This is above lactate threshold.

Two problems, one fix:

  • Compensatory eating: High-intensity work drives post-exercise appetite +12 to 24h (PMC6213307). PJ's Monday-Wednesday over-eating patterns correlate with heavy sessions.
  • NPDR active risk: Max HR >150 with proliferative-adjacent retinopathy is a documented risk factor for retinal hemorrhage. This is not a performance question — it is a vision-preservation question.

Fix: 180 − age = 143 max; conservative ceiling 138 bpm given NPDR. Use HR strap, not wrist. If HR climbs above 138, slow tempo, lengthen rest to 90 sec between sets, drop load 10%. 80% of training in this zone.

Maffetone MAF protocol; PMC6213307 (high-intensity → compensatory intake RCT).

D2. Preserve strength stimulus without high HR

  • Sets of 8–12 reps at RPE 7 (2–3 reps in reserve), not to failure.
  • Long rest (90–120 sec) between sets keeps HR down.
  • Exhale sharply on exertion — never Valsalva. NPDR reminder every session.
  • Prioritise compound lifts (weekly volume) over high-rep pump work (drives HR + eye pressure).

D3. Cardio switch

Replace tempo runs with Zone 2 walks/incline treadmill at HR 125–138. Distance target 5–7 km at slow pace. This preserves fat oxidation adaptation without triggering appetite compensation or eye risk.

5. What Is Probably Not the Problem

Not the problem: Calorie intake

Weekly deficits average 5,270 cal (target 6,100). This is a real deficit. The issue is what happens with those calories once inside a metformin-suppressed, protein-starved, cortisol-elevated body.

Not the problem: Discipline / willpower

4–5 gym sessions/week. 14,144 steps/day average. Detailed logging. Consistent kriya. PJ is executing at a level most people cannot sustain. Framing this as an effort problem is inaccurate and unhelpful.

Not the problem: Exercise volume

14k steps + 5 sessions/week is above the 75th percentile for adults. More volume will worsen the muscle catabolism, not fix it. The exercise fix is lower intensity, not more.

Not the problem: Thyroid / GI

No symptoms in logs (constipation, cold intolerance, hair loss, energy crashes decoupled from meals). Reasonable to check TSH once during Week 4 labs, but not the primary hypothesis.

The real problem, restated: Structural protein deficiency imposed by kidney disease + metformin's mTOR blunting + possible beta-blocker + cannabis-driven ghrelin surges + midnight circadian violations + defended visceral fat with no sustained clean-deficit window. Six mechanistic forces, not one behavioural one.

The path forward: Fix medication (Vector A), floor protein every day (Vector B), remove the two erasure events — cannabis + midnight food (Vector C), drop training intensity to preserve eyes and reduce compensatory eating (Vector D). Measure with the right instruments (Vector E).
